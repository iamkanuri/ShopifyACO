# STANDARD RUN 1 — Coffee Standard v1.0, executed against real stores

**The first time an AisleLens standard has been executed against anything.** Everything in
v3.0 CP0–CP4 exists so this could happen: before G-09 there was no way to hand a pinned
contract to the public path at all, so the questions asked of a public URL were generated by
a heuristic — precisely the private rubric a published standard exists to replace.

De-identified: stores are labelled by category role, not by hostname. Raw hosts stay in the
gitignored capture index.

## 1. THE HEADLINE FINDING — the run measured the standard *and* the harness, and the
## harness lost

**11 of the 25 captured products are not coffee.** They are t-shirts, mugs, a cocktail
shaker, a tote bag, drinking glasses, caramels and an espresso machine.

The capture takes the **first product handle in each store's own ordering** — arbitrary by
design, so the sample is not biased toward pages that already look good to this engine. For
a coffee roaster, that first handle is very often merchandise.

And **G-10 is open**: a standard cannot gate its own applicability. `applicability` is a
documentation field that nothing enforces, so a compiled requirement list fires **every
entry on every product**. So the raw numbers below are, in part, a measurement of whether a
tote bag states an organic certification.

This is exactly the failure `ENGINE_CONTRACT.md §7` warns about in the abstract:

> *"A fifty-question standard applied uniformly is [the `cruelty_free` failure] with a
> version number on it. `applicability` is the field that prevents it."*

The first execution of a real standard turned that warning into a measurement. **G-10 is
not a nice-to-have; it is a precondition for a category-scoped standard producing a
meaningful number.** That is the most useful thing this run established.

Both scorings are reported below. Classification uses the signal order G-10 itself
requires and the engine already uses — **`product_type` authoritative, `title` fallback,
never tags**:

| class | n | examples |
|---|---|---|
| coffee | 9 | `Coffee`, `Coffee Blends`, `Coffee - Limited`, `Single Origin` |
| not coffee | 11 | `Merch`, `Merchandise`, `Espresso Machine`, apparel, glassware |
| unknown | 5 | no `product_type`, title not decisive |

### 2c. Entries that should be reclassified `not_discriminating`

Outside the 15–85% band on the coffee-only subset, i.e. carrying ~no information:

`FORMAT-002` · `WEIGHT-001` · `CERT-001` · `CERT-002` · `SOURCE-001` · `IDENT-001`
— **six of ten.** (On all 25: five of ten.)

**The certification result is real, and it was checked rather than assumed.** A 100% fail
rate on `organic` across 25 coffee stores looks like a matcher defect, so the evidence index
was searched directly: the word `organic` appears in the extracted product evidence of
**zero of 25** stores, and `fair trade` in **zero of 25**. The rows are correct. What they
are not is *informative*: an assertion that every store fails separates no one.

`SOURCE-001` needs a footnote. `single origin` **does** appear for 6 of 25 stores — but in
5 of those 6 it is in the **shipping-policy surface** and is a collection or subscription
name (`"Single Origin Series"`, `"Single-Origin Subscription - Two Bags"`), i.e. site
navigation. The claim branch correctly refuses policy-surface evidence. **That is v2.9's
policy-chrome guard working exactly as designed on live copy** — a nice independent
confirmation of a fix made for a different reason.

## 3. Every `pass_evidenced` row was audited individually — 43 rows

A false pass under a **named standard** is worse than one under a generated task, because
the standard is the thing that is supposed to be citable. All 43 were read with the full
untruncated evidence sentence, not the 180-character rendered quote.

**Two confirmed false positives.** Both **pre-existing** — nothing in v3.0 touches timing
matching or quantity aboutness — and, critically, **neither appears in the v2.9 audit of
507 pass rows across 172 general-sample stores.**

### FP-1 — a ZIP code satisfies the delivery row's digit requirement

> *"Shipping times vary depending on your proximity to our Los Angeles origin zip code:
> 90038."*

The row rendered: **"A dispatch or delivery window is stated."** The sentence says times
**vary** and commits to nothing. A shopper learns nothing about when their coffee arrives.

Mechanism, isolated by a minimal pair: `shipping times` is a `TIMING_TERMS_NEEDING_DIGIT`
term, and the only digits present are the **postcode**. Strip them —
*"Shipping times vary depending on your proximity to our origin."* — and the row correctly
returns `not_proven`. The postcode alone carries the pass.

This is the same weakness `requireDigit` had before `dimensions` got a `valueGuard`
("Available in 3 colors" satisfying a measurement). **Delivery has no equivalent guard.**

### FP-2 — a brewing recipe read as the product's own weight

> *"…Equal amounts (by weight) filtered hot water (200-205 degrees) and ice Fresh coffee
> (just shy of 1/4 cup) for 4 ounces water and 4 ounces ice…"*

The row rendered: **"A weight or measurement is stated in readable text."** The quoted
quantities are the **water and the ice** in an iced-coffee method, not the bag's mass.

`nonProductQuantity` already vetoes this class through `USAGE_VERB` — *"Brew with 8 ounces
of water."* and the pinned *"Steep in 8 oz of hot water"* both correctly fail. But the veto
needs a **verb**, and a bare preposition (*"for 4 ounces water"*) introduces the same
quantity with nothing to match. The gap is the frame, not the concept.

**Both are pinned in the adversarial corpus with their minimal pairs and are deliberately
NOT patched here.** A term added to a veto list without an independent adversarial pass is
how v2.9's `case` regression reached a real watch store.

### The rest of the 43

Everything else is a true pass. Worth noting positively:

- **23 of 43 render no quote** and say so honestly — variant-option and spec-block
  evidence ("in a specification block rather than a sentence we can quote"). Correct, and
  the disclosure is the reason it is not a false pass.
- **Dispatch-vs-delivery wording holds.** Several delivery passes quote *processing* time
  (*"All orders are processed within 2-3 business days"*). The standard's label is
  "A **dispatch or delivery** window is stated", so these are honest — the v2.2 defect
  where "ships within a week" passed as a delivery window is not reproduced, because the
  standard's own wording is accurate about what it tests.

## 4. What this establishes, and what it does not

**Established.**

1. A published, versioned, content-hashed standard **can be compiled and executed against
   real public product URLs**, and the result carries the standard's identity so a citation
   like *"fails ALS-COFFEE-1.0-IDENT-001"* resolves to a specific text. That was not
   possible before this session.
2. The compile path is real: 10/10 entries mapped to engine `Requirement`s with no errors,
   no duplicate labels, and the `requires_store_access` collapse correctly disabled so a
   conformance list is complete.
3. **Predicted discrimination was mostly wrong, and wrong in one direction**: 8 of 10 bands
   missed, 7 of those 8 *high* — the standard's author systematically over-estimated how
   much coffee stores publish. Bands are hypotheses and this is what testing them looks
   like.
4. **Six of ten entries carry ~no information** on real coffee pages and should be
   reclassified `not_discriminating` — including all three certification/provenance claims.

**Not established.**

- Nothing about whether these stores' coffee *is* organic, fair-trade or single-origin. The
  engine adjudicates **evidence availability, never product truth**, and a 100% fail rate
  means the page does not say — not that the claim is false.
- The coffee-only rates rest on **n=9**. Directional only.
- `posture.independently_applied` remains **false**. A standard applied only by its author
  is a rubric with a version number.

---

## 5. Recommendations, in order

1. **Build G-10 (applicability gating) before publishing any conformance result.** §1 is the
   argument, and it is now measured rather than predicted. The predicate must **report which
   entries it excluded and why**, and a standard whose predicate excludes everything must be
   a loud error — the `INCOMPLETE` model in `src/measure/completion.ts`.
2. **Reclassify the six entries in §2c** to `not_discriminating`, or rewrite them. Three of
   them are the certification entries, which suggests the *executable* form of a
   certification question ("does the page say the word") is not the question a buyer asks —
   which is the argument for G-04 (register resolution), not for a better term list.
3. **Fix the sampling before re-measuring.** The first-handle rule is right for a general
   fitness sample and wrong for a category-scoped standard. A category run should select the
   first product whose `product_type` satisfies the standard's applicability.
4. **Give `delivery` a value guard.** FP-1 is the `requireDigit` weakness that every other
   digit-bearing requirement has already had closed.
